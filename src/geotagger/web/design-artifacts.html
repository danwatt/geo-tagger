Behavior: opens on focus, refetches 180 ms after each keystroke (stale responses discarded by request version). Keys — ↑ / ↓ move the cursor, Enter selects, Esc closes. Options use role="option" with aria-selected; the input carries role="combobox", aria-expanded, aria-controls, aria-activedescendant, and aria-describedby pointing at the help line. Editing the text clears the selection and resets the help line.

Date range picker — live on Search and Geocode

Built as date-range-picker.js + date-range-picker.css. On Search it replaced a bare <input type="date"> pair (native OS picker, ignored the dark theme, gave no idea where photos actually are); on Geocode it replaced the 📊 Date range button and its canvas histogram modal. Timesync keeps its own inline strip — it scrubs a working set rather than filtering, and is left alone. One trigger field, one popover carrying presets, typed bounds, the histogram scrubber, and day-precision calendars — coarse and fine selection in the same surface, no modal.

Volume is a list of {date, count} rows: the whole library from /api/stats/dates by default (Search), or the caller's own loaded scope (Geocode passes the photos already in memory and pushes a new set on every scope change). Daily counts drive the density dots, the exact in-range total, and — bucketed — the histogram bars, so nothing is fetched while the panel is open.

Two commit modes. Search runs in draft mode: the panel edits a copy and only Apply calls back, matching a page with its own Search button. Geocode runs in live mode: every edit filters the loaded photos immediately and the footer button is just Done, because waiting for Apply to filter photos already on screen would feel broken.

Typed bounds validate on blur. An inverted or unparseable range marks the offending field and blocks Apply; it never silently swaps the two, since which one the user meant is a guess.

Behavior

Three ways in, one state. Preset, scrubber drag, and calendar click all write the same {from, to}; every surface re-renders from it. Picking a preset highlights it in the rail; dragging or clicking a day switches the rail to Custom range.

Calendars follow the scrubber. The twin months track the range start, so a coarse drag lands you on the right month and the fine click is one move away. Nav arrows disable where the pair would overlap.

Counts are exact. Preset counts and the footer total are binary searches over the daily counts loaded once at startup, so they are real totals rather than month-rounded estimates — and cost nothing per interaction.

Applied on Apply. The panel is a draft; Apply (or Enter in a bound field) commits it, and Esc or a click outside closes and discards. On Search that commit only stages the range — the page keeps its explicit Search button, like every other filter in the form. A page that filters live would re-query on the same event.

Keyboard. Trigger is a button with aria-haspopup="dialog"; the panel is a focus-trapped role="dialog". In the calendar, arrows move by day, PageUp/PageDown by month, Enter sets the near bound. Scrubber handles are role="slider" with aria-valuetext carrying the readable date, so they are usable without a mouse — the one thing the current double-slider cannot do.

Calendars follow the edited bound. An end date parks on the right calendar and a start date on the left, so editing one bound rarely scrolls the other out of view.

The histogram adapts to the scope. Three months or less — a loaded month on Geocode — gets one bar per day, since a single month-wide bar says nothing. Anything longer gets one bar per month, so a 20-year library doesn't try to draw 7,000 bars into 400px. Bars are flex-weighted by their day count, so the axis stays linear in time across months of different lengths, and ticks label months or years to match, thinned to at most eight labels.

The rail earns its place or leaves. Presets that would match nothing are dropped rather than shown at zero, and a scope left with only “Any date” loses the rail entirely — Reset already does that job. A one-month Geocode scope therefore shows just the main column.

Known gaps. The old Geocode canvas stacked each bar in camera colours, showing what the Show filter was hiding; the shared histogram plots scope volume only, so that breakdown is gone. An “Undated only” preset is not offered either — /api/search has no way to ask for photos with no timestamp. Timesync could still adopt the handle and band styling for its inline strip.
